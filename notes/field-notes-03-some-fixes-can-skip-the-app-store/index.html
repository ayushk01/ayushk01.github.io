Field Notes #03: Some fixes can skip the App Store

Store releases versus over-the-air updates, explained as parcels through customs and letters straight to the phone, and why skipping customs means becoming your own.

Field Notes is a series of short, plain-language explainers on how software is built. Each one starts with a picture and ends with a rule you can use.

Phone apps get updated in two ways, and the difference is a lot like posting a parcel versus sending a letter.

A fix stuck in review isn’t a fix yet

The person with the broken screen doesn’t care that the fix is written. They care that it’s on their phone.

Two lanes to the phone

Through the store is a parcel through customs. Apple or Google checks it first, which can take days. Over the air is a letter straight to the phone: no customs, just minutes.

Letters for instructions, parcels for parts

Instructions are what the app shows and does: screens, forms, text, fixes in the app’s JavaScript. Parts are what the app is built from: camera access, new libraries, phone settings. Engineers call the letter an over-the-air (OTA) update, and frameworks like React Native make it possible.

One caveat: Apple’s rules allow over-the-air updates only as long as they don’t change what the app is for. A letter can fix and tweak. It can’t turn your app into a different app.

The catch: no customs means nobody checks

One bad letter reaches every phone at once. And a letter written for new parts, arriving at a phone with old parts, crashes the app.

Be your own customs

  • The same checklist as a store release.
  • An undo you’ve actually practised.
  • Match every letter to the app version it’s meant for.
  • “It’s only a small change” is never a reason to skip a step.

The rule

The fast lane only works if it’s boring.
