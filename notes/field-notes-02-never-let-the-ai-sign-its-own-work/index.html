Field Notes #02: Never let the AI sign its own work

The simplest safety pattern for AI that writes anything important: treat it like a new intern. It drafts, a person signs, and nothing is saved until it's signed.

Field Notes is a series of short, plain-language explainers on how software is built. Each one starts with a picture and ends with a rule you can use.

AI is fast, and it’s usually right. But when it’s wrong, it’s wrong with total confidence.

In a shopping app, a wrong answer is a bad suggestion. In a clinic, a wrong medicine name in a patient’s record isn’t a typo. It’s harm. The same mistake can cost very different amounts, and the higher the cost, the less the AI should decide alone.

Treat the AI like a new intern

A new intern writes the first version. A senior reads it, fixes it, and signs it. Only signed work goes into the record. Engineers call this human-in-the-loop. The idea is simpler than the name: the AI drafts, a person decides.

You can’t promise the AI will never be wrong. No model is perfect. But you can promise it will never be the one who decides. A rule like “nothing is saved until a person signs” can be perfect, and you can test it.

The catch: slow checks get skipped

Busy people sign whatever is in front of them if checking takes too long. So the real work isn’t the AI. It’s making “read, fix, sign” much faster than “write it yourself”. If fixing the draft is slower than starting over, the whole thing fails.

A free bonus

Every time a person corrects a draft, you learn exactly where the AI is weak. The check that keeps people safe also tells you what to improve next.

The rule

The AI drafts. A human signs. In medicine, law, money, anywhere a mistake can hurt someone.
