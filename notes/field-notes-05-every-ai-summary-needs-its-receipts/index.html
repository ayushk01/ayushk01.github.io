Field Notes #05: Every AI summary needs its receipts

Why an AI summary should carry the exact sources behind every claim, explained with expense reports, and what I learned building Verbatim.

Field Notes is a series of short, plain-language explainers on how software is built. Each one starts with a picture and ends with a rule you can use.

AI summaries always sound sure

“Users love the new checkout.” Says who? How many? In what words? A summary without its sources is just a confident opinion.

Accounts won’t pay without a receipt

Nobody’s offended by that rule. It’s how trust works when you can’t check everything yourself. AI summaries deserve the same rule.

So I built Verbatim

Paste in reviews, support tickets or interview notes. Verbatim groups them into ranked themes, and every theme carries the exact quotes behind it, one click away. Try the demo with sample data.

The source sits right under the claim

The summary can’t quietly drift, because the reader can check it on the spot. A wrong label gets exposed, not hidden. Engineers call this grounding: tying every claim to the evidence it came from.

Receipts aren’t free

More tokens and a little more waiting per theme. More interface than a bullet list. And some humility: sometimes the quotes show the AI’s label is wrong, and the user should see that. One boring rule on top: the AI is called from the server, so no key ever reaches the browser.

The rule

No receipt, no claim.
