Field Notes #04: Not all AI mistakes are equal

Why one accuracy score hides the errors that matter, explained with a wedding card: check the date twice, the poem once.

Field Notes is a series of short, plain-language explainers on how software is built. Each one starts with a picture and ends with a rule you can use.

Think about proofreading a wedding card. A typo in the poem? Nobody notices. A wrong date? Every guest turns up on the wrong day.

”95% accurate” hides the real question

Most teams measure AI output with one number. But the question that matters is which 5% is wrong. A wrong word in a summary is noise. A wrong number, date, amount or dose is harm. One average score treats them the same.

Proofread like it’s a wedding card

Nobody remembers a typo in the poem. Everybody remembers turning up on the wrong day.

Sort every field by what a mistake would cost

  • Low: wording, tone, summaries.
  • Medium: names, categories, labels.
  • High: numbers, dates, doses, money, and the word “no”.

Engineers call this a field’s blast radius.

The smallest word can do the most damage

“No history of diabetes” and “History of diabetes” differ by one word. Both read perfectly, and AI is very good at reading perfectly.

Check hardest where it hurts most

  1. Score each field separately, not one average.
  2. Match high-risk fields to the source, word for word.
  3. Watch the small words: no, not, never, without.
  4. Point the reviewer’s eyes at the risky fields first.

Engineers call this field-level evaluation.

The rule

Check the date twice. The poem once.
