## The product

WoodVer is the Indian arm of an Italian wood-coatings manufacturer (Verinlegno). I built the original version of their loyalty-rewards Android app — sign up, accrue points on purchases, redeem against products in a catalog.

A small freelance engagement, but a real one. Scoped with the client, built solo, signed and shipped under their developer account, and supported through early users. The listing remains live on [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.WoodverCoatingBasiq360.app); the current version has since been replaced by a successor build the client commissioned after this one.

## What I built

- **Authentication and onboarding** — phone-based sign-up wired to their existing customer records.
- **Points wallet** with transaction history and tier rules.
- **Redemption catalog** — products and rewards the client could update without a redeploy.
- **A simple admin surface** so the client team could manage the catalog, approve redemptions, and pull basic activity reports.

## Stack notes

- **Mobile:** Flutter / Dart, single Android target.
- **Backend / data:** Firebase (Auth + Firestore + Cloud Functions for the redemption flow).
- **Distribution:** Google Play, signed and submitted by me.

## What I'd do differently today

Two honest reflections — both about being early in my career and matching tools to the constraints in front of me at the time, not in hindsight.

**Firebase was right for the timeline, not for the long run.** Firestore + Cloud Functions got the app shipped in a fraction of the time a "proper" backend would have, which was the correct trade for a v1 freelance build. But once a rewards system has real money behind it, you start wanting things Firestore doesn't naturally give you: transactional consistency across multi-document operations (point debits + redemption records), straightforward audit trails, and reporting queries that don't fight the document model. Today I'd still consider Firebase for the MVP, but I'd be clearer with the client from day one about what the migration looks like once volume justifies it.

**Single-platform Android-only was a constraint, not a choice.** The client's customer base was Android-heavy enough that iOS wasn't worth the budget at launch, which was fair. But it left no easy path to "actually we do want an iOS build now" without re-architecting. If I were scoping the same project today, I'd default to a cross-platform setup (Flutter or React Native) even for an Android-first launch, just to keep the option open.

The successor build the client commissioned is, I think, a sign the original was solid enough to be worth investing in — and the things I'd change are exactly the things that show up *because* something shipped.
